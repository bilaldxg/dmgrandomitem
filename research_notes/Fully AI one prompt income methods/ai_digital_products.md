## Amazon KDP AI books (low-content and AI-written): disclosure, upload limits, crackdowns, royalties, saturation

### Takeaway
KDP still allows fully AI-generated books. Disclosure to Amazon is required but is not shown to buyers. The fight is now about volume and quality. The upload cap set in 2023 (3 titles a day) seems to have been tightened in 2026 to a weekly per-format cap, but sources conflict on the number. AI books have flooded the market: one study found the catalog in its sample grew 38x while revenue grew 9x. Expected earnings per title for undifferentiated AI books are low.

### Cited Findings
- **Disclosure rule:** at publishing, KDP asks whether the book contains AI-generated text, images (including the cover) or translations. "AI-generated" content must be disclosed. "AI-assisted" content (you wrote it, and AI only edited, brainstormed or error-checked) does not need disclosure. The disclosure is not shown on the product page and does not affect royalties or eligibility. — [publishing.co.uk](https://publishing.co.uk/guides/kdp-ai-content-disclosure/); [scribecount](https://scribecount.com/author-resource/protecting-books/kdp-ai-content-policy-the-compliance-guide) [blog]
- AI artwork in activity and coloring books also needs disclosure. One source says Amazon gives "enhanced quality reviews" to AI-flagged image-heavy titles such as coloring books. — [univers.studio](https://univers.studio/blog/kdp-ai-content-policy-2026/); [writersofthewest](https://writersofthewest.net/blog/amazon-kdp-ai-content-policy/) [blog; unverified against Amazon]
- **2023 upload cap:** the Authors Guild reported that Amazon added to its KDP generative AI policy and capped daily self-publishing uploads. The widely cited figure is 3 new titles per day per account (dated September 2023 per my training knowledge). — [Authors Guild](https://authorsguild.org/news/amazon-adds-to-kdp-generative-ai-policy-caps-daily-self-publishing-uploads/) (page not fetchable; snippet only)
- **2026 change (conflicting):** one 2026 guide quotes KDP's Create a Book page, read October 2026: "Creation limit: Up to 2 titles per book format each week." Another describes 2 new titles created (from Sept 21, 2026) and 2 submitted for publication (from Oct 5, 2026) per format each week. A third source cites the Paperback Submission Guidelines for a cap of 10 per format per week and says high-volume publishers can ask KDP for an exception. Other 2026 articles still repeat "3 per day". — [kdpbook.io](https://kdpbook.io/guides/can-you-publish-ai-generated-books-on-kdp); [aiwritebook](https://aiwritebook.com/en/blog/ai-generated-books-amazon-legal/); [zarifautomates](https://www.zarifautomates.com/blog/amazon-kdp-ai-generated-content-policy) [all blogs; conflict unresolved]
- **Enforcement claims:** blogs say Amazon tightened AI disclosure enforcement in April 2026 and improved AI-content detection. They also say Amazon removes "spammy, duplicative, or low-effort" low-content books, with triggers including duplicate interiors, minimal differentiation, mass-generated uploads and keyword-stuffed metadata. — [writeo.app](https://writeo.app/news/amazon-kdp-ai-disclosure-enforcement-2026); [pubnook](https://pubnook.com/article/amazon-kdp-low-content-book-policy-2026-whats-allowed-and-what-gets-removed); [xpresspublisher](https://blog.xpresspublisher.com/ai-self-publishing-amazon-kdp-policy-2026/) [blog; no Amazon primary source found]
- **Hardest evidence of the flood:** a study led by Stony Brook University looked at 14,419 self-published genre-fiction books on Amazon from 2023 to 2026. About 20% had substantial AI-generated text, and 2,880 titles had more than 25% of their prose flagged by the Pangram detector. The catalog grew 38.3x over the period while quarterly revenue grew only 8.9x. The study is not yet peer-reviewed. — [Fortune, 2026-09-14](https://fortune.com/2026/09/14/ai-slop-books-amazon-marketplace-publishing-human-authors/); [UMich SI (Dhillon)](https://www.si.umich.edu/about-umsi/news/dhillon-ai-generated-books-are-squeezing-human-authors-out-online-sales); [Entangled Text](https://www.entangledtext.com/news/study-finds-ai-generated-books-are-crowding-amazon-and-cutting-author-pay)
- Researchers documented more than 517 nonfiction children's books on Amazon as partly or fully AI-generated. When the list was shared with Amazon, 198 were removed. Amazon told Fortune that it takes "proactive and reactive measures" against content that violates its guidelines, "whether AI-generated or not". — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/14/ai-slop-books-amazon-marketplace-publishing-human-authors/); [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/amazons-ai-generated-childrens-books-flood-gift-piles)
- One industry piece says more than 30% of new KDP titles in 2026 carry an AI disclosure. — [the-decoder / aggregator](https://the-decoder.com/ai-generated-books-are-flooding-amazon-and-tanking-sales-for-human-authors/) [lower-rigor source]
- **One-prompt tooling:** coloring-book generators batch-produce pages with consistent characters and line weight and export print-ready PDFs for KDP and Etsy (colorin.ai, createcoloringbooks.ai, kdpbuilder.com, mycoloringpages.ai). One vendor warns that commercial or resale rights are not granted by default. — [colorin.ai](https://colorin.ai/); [createcoloringbooks.ai](https://www.createcoloringbooks.ai/); [kdpbuilder](https://kdpbuilder.com/ai-coloring-book-creator); [mycoloringpages.ai](https://www.mycoloringpages.ai/kdp-coloring-book-generator); [everalice](https://everalice.studio/ai-coloring-book-creator) [vendor]
- **Royalties** [training-knowledge, pre-2026; not re-verified]: eBooks pay 70% between $2.99 and $9.99 (minus delivery cost) or 35% outside that range. Paperbacks pay 60% of list price minus printing cost. Low-content paperbacks priced at $6.99–$9.99 typically net roughly $1–$3 per sale.

### Inferences
- KDP AI books are close to one-prompt: generators produce the PDF interior, AI makes the cover, and the LLM writes the metadata. The upload step and disclosure questionnaire are still manual, and a cap of 2 per format per week (if confirmed) kills the volume strategy. An account can produce at most about 100 paperbacks a year, so each title has to be curated.
- The 38x catalog versus 9x revenue ratio is the best quantitative proxy for saturation: average revenue per title in that sample fell by about 77%. Undifferentiated AI coloring books are likely the most saturated sub-niche, but I found no hard count.
- AI-generated books cannot be protected by copyright (see USCO section), so anyone can clone a successful AI coloring book.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch KDP's official Content Guidelines or Create a Book page, so the current upload cap (2, 3 or 10, and per day or per week) is unverified.
- No primary Amazon announcement of 2025–2026 crackdowns was found.
- No verified income data for AI low-content publishers (r/KDP threads did not appear in search results), and no count of AI coloring books.

## Etsy digital downloads (AI printables, wall art, planners, clipart, Notion/Canva templates) and Etsy POD: Creativity Standards, AI disclosure, fees, earnings

### Takeaway
Etsy allows AI products, but sellers must disclose AI use. AI items fall under "Designed by" rather than "Made by". Mass-produced resale is banned, and enforcement seems to have increased in 2026. Fees run about 10% before Offsite Ads. Printable wall art is widely described as saturated with near-identical AI prints. The median and typical shop earnings quoted are low, and none of them are verified.

### Cited Findings
- Etsy replaced its old "Handmade" framework with Creativity Standards (dated July 2024 by one source). These standards make sellers label items by degree of human involvement: Made by, Designed by, Handpicked, Sourced by. Items made from a seller's AI prompts count as "Designed by a seller". — [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/etsy-shopping-rules-ai-warning-online-shoppers-1923399); [promptlesspress](https://www.promptlesspress.com/blog-etsy-ai-policy-2026-digital-products) [blog]
- Rule as quoted by blogs: "Sellers must disclose within their listing description if an item is created with the use of AI." The Creativity Standards page reportedly reads "Last updated on Jun 10, 2025". The disclosure requirement applies to the product itself, not to AI-edited product photos. — [ngini](https://ngini.com/en-us/blog/etsy-ai-disclosure-policy-2026-explained); [lzypod](https://lzypod.com/blog/etsy-ai-policy-what-sellers-need-to-know/); [nightjar](https://nightjar.so/help-desk/do-i-need-to-disclose-that-my-product-images-are-ai-generated-on-etsy-or-shopify) [blog; official page could not be fetched]
- Etsy has said it saw "an increase in mass produced and generic goods from retailer sites", which is why it tightened the rules. Penalties range from removing a listing to suspending the shop for repeated or large-scale violations. — [bulkmockup](https://www.bulkmockup.com/etsy-policy-changes/); [growtsy](https://www.growtsy.com/etsy-ai-policy) [blog]
- **Unverified 2026 enforcement claims:** one blog says enforcement started January 14, 2026, and another says "12,000+ listings removed, ~8,500 warnings issued in Q1 2026". One guide claims Etsy banned prompt bundles entirely. None of these could be checked against Etsy announcements. — [artomate](https://www.artomate.app/blog/etsy-ai-disclosure-policy-2026); [aliteq](https://aliteq.com/how-to-sell-ai-digital-products-etsy); [aiprofitmode](https://aiprofitmode.com/how-to-sell-on-promptbase.html) [blog; treat with caution]
- **Fees (2026):** $0.20 listing or renewal fee, 6.5% transaction fee, and US payment processing of 3% + $0.25. Offsite Ads cost 15% for shops under $10k a year (opt-out allowed) and 12% for shops over $10k (mandatory), capped at $100 per order. The typical total is about 10% without ads and 22–25% on an ad-attributed sale. — [Gelato](https://www.gelato.com/blog/etsy-fees); [Craftybase](https://app.craftybase.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-etsy-fees); [checkoutpage](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/etsy-fees) [secondary but consistent]
- **Saturation:** "printable wall art is genuinely saturated — thousands of near-identical AI-generated prints were listed in 2024–2026, and most earn nothing". "Planner printable" returns 500,000+ results, and the claim is that generic keywords are saturated while narrow niches are not. — [penluma](https://penluma.com/blog/make-money-with-ai/16-print-on-demand-etsy/); [anlyzo](https://anlyzo.com/blog/is-selling-printables-on-etsy-saturated); [growingyourcraft](https://www.growingyourcraft.com/blog/etsy-passive-income-selling-printables) [blog]
- **Earnings (unverified estimates):** one figure puts median seller revenue at about $574 a month. Another claims "70% earn under $100/mo, top sellers make $5K+". — [listifyai](https://www.listifyai.net/blog/how-much-do-etsy-sellers-make-2026); [insightagent](https://www.insightagent.app/guides/how-much-do-etsy-sellers-make) [vendor estimates]
- **One-prompt tooling:** Printify connects to Etsy so listings sync and fulfillment is automated. A Substack guide describes an image generator → upscaler → Printify chain using Printify's native Make.com module. — [Printify](https://printify.com/sell-on-etsy-drop-shipping/); [Substack](https://automatedincomelifestyle.substack.com/p/ai-automated-print-on-demand-a-step)

### Inferences
- A fully automated pipeline (LLM picks a niche → image model → upscaler → mockup → Printify or Etsy API listing) is technically possible. Etsy's policy against mass-produced, generic listings and its spam enforcement make a high-volume one-prompt pipeline the riskiest pattern on the platform. The $0.20 listing fee also means 1,000 listings cost $200 every four months in renewals.
- Digital printables are pure margin after about 10% in fees, but discoverability is the bottleneck. In a saturated category, near-zero sales for most new AI shops is the base case.

### Gaps
- The text of Etsy's official Creativity Standards could not be fetched. Whether Etsy has a formal AI checkbox or field, or only a description disclosure, is unconfirmed.
- No r/EtsySellers threads with real AI printable revenue appeared. No Etsy-published count of AI listings or removals was found.

## Print-on-demand (Merch by Amazon, Redbubble, TeePublic, Printful/Printify): tiers, AI rules, trademark risk, earnings

### Takeaway
None of the major POD platforms ban AI art outright. The binding constraints are IP and anti-spam rules, plus Merch by Amazon's slot tiers. A reported June 2026 change ties Merch royalties to external traffic, which hurts passive sellers who rely on organic Amazon search. Trademark risk is the main hazard of AI-generated designs.

### Cited Findings
- **Merch by Amazon tiers:** sellers start with 10 design slots and unlock higher tiers (25, 100, 500 and so on) through cumulative sales. Promotion also weighs account health, return rates, violation history, conversion and "trademark cleanliness". A trailing-12-month review can demote a seller. Listings that haven't sold in 18 months are removed. — [prinil tiers](https://prinil.com/amazon-merch-tier-system-explained-2026/); [amzprep](https://amzprep.com/amazon-merch-on-demand/); [Printify](https://printify.com/knowledge-hub/is-amazon-merch-on-demand-worth-it/) [blog]
- **June 1, 2026 royalty change (reported):** Merch moved from a flat royalty to three tiers based on external traffic driven through Amazon Attribution. Sellers with only organic traffic earn less. Reported payouts: about $2.44–$5.27 per tee, or "$1.93 to $7+ per shirt" depending on price tier. — [merchtitans](https://merchtitans.com/blog/amazon-merch-royalty-changes-june-2026); [prinil guide](https://prinil.com/amazon-merch-on-demand-complete-guide-2026/) [blog; not verified with Amazon]
- **AI on Merch:** AI images are generally allowed if you created them. Filters flag designs "substantially similar" to existing works even when the prompt was original. One blog claims Amazon rolled out automated AI-content detection in Q4 2025. Amazon removes infringing content proactively, without a rights-holder complaint. — [ecommerce-platforms](https://ecommerce-platforms.com/articles/merch-by-amazon-review); [bebolddigital](https://www.bebolddigital.com/blog/amazon-merch-on-demand); [nexscope](https://www.nexscope.ai/blog/amazon-merch-on-demand) [blog; conflicting]
- **Redbubble and TeePublic:** neither bans AI art. Redbubble requires that you own or have permission for what you upload and that it does not infringe copyright, trademark or likeness. Its User Agreement (updated May 15, 2026) reportedly does not mention AI. TeePublic does not explicitly mention AI. Reports of a Redbubble "AI checkbox" with a cap of 5 AI uploads a day, and of a TeePublic "Made with AI" tag, are disputed and unconfirmed. Uploading hundreds of near-identical designs is treated as spam. Some sellers report sales dropping after being moved to TeePublic's "Apprentice" tier, but no link to AI is confirmed. — [profitlab360](https://profitlab360.com/blog/can-you-sell-ai-art-on-redbubble); [trendlytic](https://www.trendlytic.io/blog/can-you-sell-ai-art-on-redbubble); [merchbanao](https://merchbanao.com/blog/ai-art-for-print-on-demand/); [autokeyworder](https://autokeyworder.com/blog/teepublic-vs-redbubble/) [blog]

### Inferences
- **Trademark and copyright risk:** image models readily reproduce trademarked characters, logos and phrases. A one-prompt pipeline without an IP filter is very likely to accumulate strikes, and on Merch that blocks tier promotion and can end the account. A human (or at least a trademark-database check such as USPTO TESS) is still needed.
- Merch's 10-slot start and sales-gated tiers make it structurally impossible to scale AI volume fast. Printify and Printful on Etsy or Shopify have no slot cap, but sellers carry ad and traffic costs.

### Gaps
- No official Redbubble or TeePublic AI policy page, and no verified seller earnings for AI POD.
- No official Amazon confirmation of the June 2026 royalty tiers.

## AI stock images, video and vectors (Adobe Stock, Shutterstock, Freepik, Getty): policies and earnings per download

### Takeaway
Adobe Stock and Freepik accept labelled AI content. Getty bans it. Shutterstock's contributor policy is reported as no AI submissions, though one 2026 guide disagrees. Contributor revenue at Adobe is reported to have collapsed as AI assets passed about 50% of the library and rejection rates rose. Only anecdotes and forum posts support this, not official data.

### Cited Findings
- **Adobe Stock** [official]: accepts generative AI as JPEG, AI, EPS or SVG. The "Generative AI" option must be selected at upload, and the same quality standards apply. Recognizable people need releases. AI content is barred from Illustrative Editorial and from titles that imply a newsworthy event. Weekly submission limits and caps on pending content apply. — [Adobe Stock GenAI FAQ](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/submit-your-content/submit-generative-ai-content/adobe-stock-generative-ai-faq.html); [Adobe upload guidelines](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/content-policies-guidelines/content-policies/content-upload-guidelines.html); [Adobe blog 2024-09](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2024/09/10/adobe-stock-continued-commitments-to-creators)
- Adobe pays a Firefly Contributor bonus to contributors whose content was considered for training. A payment was made on **September 17, 2026**, and the payout minimum was cut from $25 to $1 for three months for recipients. Contributors are also paid when an asset is used as a generative reference in "Customize" and the result is downloaded. — [Adobe Firefly contributor FAQ](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/submit-your-content/submit-generative-ai-content/firefly-faq.html) [official]
- The Adobe royalty is 33% on most assets and 35% on video. — [virtuall.pro](https://virtuall.pro/blog/adobe-stock-contributor) [blog, about 9 months old]
- **Collapse reports** [forum, self-reported]: one Adobe forum contributor's monthly earnings fell from about $1,000–1,200 to $250–300 after rejection rates rose. Another reported a drop of about 50%. An Adobe community expert said AI assets now make up more than half the database. — [Adobe Community thread](https://community.adobe.com/t5/stock-contributors-discussions/drastic-decrease-in-monthly-earnings/m-p/15572728); [Adobe Community "Sudden drop"](https://community.adobe.com/questions-38/sudden-drop-in-adobe-stock-downloads-anyone-else-experiencing-this-1560315)
- One AI contributor's self-report: $3,976 total over 12 months, growing from $12 in month 1 to $810 in month 12. The source is a vendor blog (a keyword-tool company). — [autokeyworder](https://autokeyworder.com/blog/ai-stock-photo-earnings-3976/) [self-reported, vendor]
- Adobe tightened submission standards in 2026, pushing contributors toward fewer, higher-intent uploads. — [pixelkeyai](https://pixelkeyai.com/blog/ai-generated-stock-submission-rules-2026) [blog]
- **Shutterstock:** its contributor help page reportedly says it "will not allow AI-generated content to be submitted by contributors for licensing". It sells output from its own generator, trained on licensed content, and pays contributors through the Contributor Fund, tied to a 20% average royalty on data-licensing revenue. Opt-out toggles were added in 2023 and split into image and video in 2024. One 2026 guide instead describes a tiered declaration system for AI-assisted and fully AI content. Shutterstock's overall revenue was near record in 2025, but its core Content segment declined. — [aimetadatacleaner](https://aimetadatacleaner.com/blog/shutterstock-ai-content-policies-2026-guide); [stockpixtagger](https://stockpixtagger.com/blog/shutterstock-contributor-guide); [pixelkeyai earnings](https://pixelkeyai.com/blog/shutterstock-contributor-earnings-drop-2026) [blog; conflict noted]
- **Getty Images** [official]: does not accept AI-generated visuals into its Creative or Editorial libraries. Visuals generated with Getty's own AI tools are labelled and not added back. Limited AI retouching that adds no new elements is allowed. — [Getty AI-Free Imagery Policy](https://www.gettyimages.com/company/ai-free-imagery-policy); [rastock](https://rastock.ai/blog/getty-images-contributor-requirements). The ban dates to September 2022. — [The Verge, 2022](https://www.theverge.com/2022/9/21/23364696/getty-images-ai-ban-generated-artwork-illustration-copyright) [training-knowledge URL]
- **Freepik:** accepts AI photos, vectors and PSDs from any tool, with a mandatory `_ai_generated` tag. Most rejections are for quality (hands, fingers, teeth, garbled text, repeating patterns). — [rastock Freepik guide](https://rastock.ai/blog/speed-up-freepik-uploads-with-ai-metadata) [blog]

### Inferences
- AI stock is one of the most automatable methods: generation, batch upscaling and LLM keywording tools (AutoKeyWorder, PixTagger, RAStock) exist. The economics have inverted, though. Unlimited supply plus buyers who can generate their own images (including through Adobe's own Firefly) means falling earnings per asset. Weekly upload caps and stricter review limit the volume strategy.
- A realistic outcome for a new AI contributor in 2026 is tens to low hundreds of dollars a month after many months and thousands of accepted assets. Only self-reports support this.

### Gaps
- No official per-download royalty averages or contributor payout totals for 2025–2026. No r/stocksubmitters aggregate data surfaced.
- Shutterstock's current contributor AI policy is unresolved because sources conflict.

## Selling prompts and AI assets (PromptBase etc.)

### Takeaway
PromptBase takes a 20% fee and caps new sellers at $4.99 per prompt. Per the more skeptical analyses, even top-ranked sellers earn only a few hundred dollars a month. Supply exceeds demand, and freely shared prompts plus better models are eroding the value of a prompt.

### Cited Findings
- PromptBase takes a 20% commission (seller keeps 80%). New accounts are capped at $4.99 per prompt until established. — [aiprofitmode](https://aiprofitmode.com/how-to-sell-on-promptbase.html); [hubpy](https://hubpy.io/blog/promptbase-guide-2026) [blog, consistent]
- PromptBase does not publish an earnings distribution. Top-40 sellers reportedly clear $200–400 a month, or $200–600 a month, from pure prompt sales. Guru guides claim $2K–$20K a month. Multiple long-term users say "more sellers than buyers". — [digiko](https://digiko.io/ai-money/selling-ai-prompts-is-a-25b-market-read-what-sellers-keep); [aiprofitmode](https://aiprofitmode.com/how-to-sell-on-promptbase.html); guru claim: [NeuraPulse](https://neuraplus-ai.github.io/blog/selling-ai-prompts-online-guide.html)
- A market-size estimate of $2.51B in 2026 (from $1.94B in 2025) is attributed to The Business Research Company. It is a market-research estimate of unclear scope. — [digiko](https://digiko.io/ai-money/selling-ai-prompts-is-a-25b-market-read-what-sellers-keep)
- Unverified claims: PromptBase cut app-credit payouts in 2025, and Etsy banned prompt bundles. — [aiprofitmode](https://aiprofitmode.com/how-to-sell-on-promptbase.html) [unverified]

### Inferences
- Prompts are the most one-prompt product of all, since an LLM can write prompts. That is exactly why they have almost no scarcity value. The guru figures ($5K–$20K a month) are not supported by any data found.

### Gaps
- No audited PromptBase or competitor (e.g., Etsy prompt-pack) sales data.

## AI-generated ebooks and courses on Gumroad, Payhip and Whop

### Takeaway
Platform fees are well documented. Gumroad charges 10% + $0.50, rising to 30% through Discover. Payhip charges 5% on its free plan. Whop charges about 3% or processor fees only. I found no credible data on earnings, refunds or saturation for AI-generated ebooks and courses. Distribution comes from the seller's own audience, not the marketplace, so a product without traffic is not "one-prompt income".

### Cited Findings
- Gumroad: 10% + $0.50 per direct sale, 30% through the Discover marketplace. Payhip: 5% (free plan), 2% (Plus, $29/mo) or 0% (Pro, $99/mo), plus about 2.9% processing. Whop: sources conflict ("no platform fee, 2.7% + $0.30" versus "3% per sale"). On a $100 sale, one example nets $91.80 on Payhip versus $86.80 on Gumroad. — [wearefounders](https://www.wearefounders.uk/best-platforms-for-selling-digital-products-in-2026/); [sellfy](https://sellfy.com/blog/best-gumroad-alternatives/); [mintavocado](https://mintavocado.com/en/payhip-review-2026/); [insightraider](https://insightraider.com/en/compare/gumroad-vs-payhip) [blog]
- Unverified claims: "8% of the top 100 Gumroad sellers migrated to Whop in 2025", and reports of held or skipped Gumroad payouts in 2025–2026. — [kourses](https://kourses.com/gumroad-alternatives/); [checkthat.ai](https://checkthat.ai/brands/gumroad/pricing) [unverified]

### Inferences
- An LLM can write a 50-page ebook or a course outline with scripts in one prompt, and AI avatar or voice tools can make the videos. Sales on these platforms depend almost entirely on the seller's external traffic (social, email, ads), which is the opposite of one-prompt. A fully AI product with no audience behind it most likely sells close to zero.

## US Copyright Office Part 2 report (Jan 2025): AI outputs and protecting AI products

### Takeaway
Purely AI-generated output, including anything made from prompts alone, is not copyrightable in the US. Sellers of one-prompt products therefore cannot stop copycats from cloning their designs, books or images. Only human-authored additions, selection and arrangement, or modifications are protectable.

### Cited Findings
- The USCO's Copyright and AI report Part 2 (Copyrightability) was released January 29, 2025. With current generally available technology, "prompts alone do not provide sufficient control" for copyright protection of outputs. Prompts function as unprotectable ideas, and many repeated revisions do not change that. — [Library of Congress newsroom](https://newsroom.loc.gov/news/copyright-office-releases-part-2-of-artificial-intelligence-report/s/f3959c36-d616-498d-b8f9-67641fd18bab) [official]; [Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/ip-updates/us-copyright-office-ai-prompts-alone-provide-insufficient-control-over-expression-to-protect-ai-generated-content.html); [Copyright Alliance](https://copyrightalliance.org/ai-report-part-2-copyrightability/)
- Perceptible human expression in an output, and creative arrangement or modification of outputs, can be protected. Selecting a single output from several is not a creative act. Wholly AI-generated material is not protected. Determinations are case by case. The Office recommended no new legislation or sui generis right. — [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/us-copyright-office-releases-part-2-of-artificial-intelligence-report-clarifying-copyrightability-of-generative-ai-outputs); [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/02/copyrightability-of-ai-outputs-us-copyright-office-analyzes-human-authorship-requirement); [Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/54731/2025-02-07-us-copyright-office-publishes-second-part-report-ai); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/copyright-office-anticipated-part-two-report-ai-addressing-copyrightability/)
- The report adopted the Authors Guild's "roulette wheel" analogy for repeated prompting. — [Authors Guild](https://authorsguild.org/news/us-copyright-office-ai-report-part-2-what-authors-should-know/)
- Related [training-knowledge, pre-2026; not re-verified]: in Thaler v. Perlmutter (D.C. Cir., March 2025), the court affirmed that the Copyright Act requires human authorship.

### Inferences
- **Business implications:** (1) A successful AI coloring book, shirt design or printable can be copied legally (trademarks and listing text aside), which speeds up saturation. (2) DMCA takedowns against copycats rest on weak ground for purely AI work. (3) The only defensible assets are a brand, an audience, human-curated compilations or arrangements, and human-edited text and art. (4) The flip side is that AI outputs can still infringe others' copyrights and trademarks, so liability is one-directional.

### Gaps

## Verified income vs guru claims, saturation, and how truly "one-prompt" each method is

### Takeaway
Nearly all earnings figures available for these methods are self-reported or come from vendors selling the tools. The few rigorous data points, the Stony Brook/Fortune KDP study and the Adobe forum collapse reports, all point to falling revenue per unit as AI supply explodes. None of the methods is truly one-prompt end to end. Generation is automated. Platform caps, disclosure forms, IP screening, quality review and traffic acquisition are not.

### Cited Findings
- KDP: 38.3x growth in titles versus 8.9x growth in revenue in a 14,419-book sample, 2023–2026. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/14/ai-slop-books-amazon-marketplace-publishing-human-authors/)
- Adobe Stock: AI is said to be more than 50% of assets, and contributors report income drops of 50–75%. — [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/stock-contributors-discussions/drastic-decrease-in-monthly-earnings/m-p/15572728)
- Etsy: "most [AI wall art prints] earn nothing", and one estimate claims 70% of sellers earn under $100 a month. — [penluma](https://penluma.com/blog/make-money-with-ai/16-print-on-demand-etsy/); [insightagent](https://www.insightagent.app/guides/how-much-do-etsy-sellers-make)
- PromptBase: top-40 sellers earn $200–400 a month (skeptical analysis), against $5K–$20K guru claims. — [digiko](https://digiko.io/ai-money/selling-ai-prompts-is-a-25b-market-read-what-sellers-keep); [NeuraPulse](https://neuraplus-ai.github.io/blog/selling-ai-prompts-online-guide.html)
- Many "income guides" are published by sellers of AI tools (keyword taggers, coloring-book generators, listing generators). This is a conflict of interest. — e.g., [autokeyworder](https://autokeyworder.com/blog/ai-stock-photo-earnings-3976/); [listifyai](https://www.listifyai.net/blog/how-much-do-etsy-sellers-make-2026)

### Inferences
Approximate one-prompt-ness and outlook (synthesis, not sourced data):

| Method | Automatable share | Main human or structural bottleneck | Startup cost | Time to first revenue | Saturation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| KDP low-content / AI books | High (generators produce interior, cover, metadata) | Weekly or daily upload caps, disclosure, quality removals, copycats | ~$0–$30/mo tools | Weeks to months | Very high |
| Merch by Amazon | High generation | 10-slot start, tier gating, trademark strikes, 2026 royalty tiers favor outside traffic | $0 (application-gated) | Months (tier climb) | Very high |
| Redbubble / TeePublic | High | Spam rules, discoverability, low margins | $0 | Months | Very high |
| Printify/Printful + Etsy or Shopify | Very high (Make.com/API pipelines) | Etsy mass-production rules, $0.20 listings, ad costs, IP | Low to moderate | Weeks to months | Very high |
| Etsy printables / clipart / templates | High | Disclosure, discoverability, about 10–25% fees | $0.20 per listing | Weeks to months | Very high (wall art, generic planners) |
| AI stock (Adobe, Freepik) | Very high (generate + auto-keyword) | Weekly caps, rejections, collapsing earnings per asset | Near $0 | 1–3 months; slow ramp | Extreme (AI more than 50% of Adobe library) |
| Prompts (PromptBase) | Total | No scarcity, $4.99 cap, buyer shortage | $0 | Weeks | Extreme |
| AI ebooks/courses (Gumroad/Payhip/Whop) | High for content | Needs external audience and traffic | $0–$99/mo | Depends on audience | Unknown (no data) |

- The pattern across platforms is the same. Supply is effectively unlimited, and platforms respond with throttles (KDP caps, Merch tiers, Adobe weekly limits), disclosure, and spam or mass-production enforcement. That pushes the viable model away from one-prompt volume and toward fewer, curated, niche or brand-backed products.
