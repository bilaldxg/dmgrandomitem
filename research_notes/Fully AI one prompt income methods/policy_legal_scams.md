## 1. The "AI passive income guru" economy: FTC/SEC/CFTC enforcement and who actually makes money

### Takeaway
Since Sept 2024 the FTC has repeatedly sued and permanently banned operators who sold "AI-powered" ecommerce stores and "passive income" coaching. Judgments run into the tens of millions of dollars, and the pattern continued into 2026 (Air AI order, March 2026). Independent evidence points one way: the reliable money in "AI side hustles" goes to the people selling courses, communities and "done-for-you" stores, not to their buyers. Course sellers' own income claims are unverified, and no platform-level earnings disclosure exists for Whop or Skool.

### Cited Findings
**Operation AI Comply (Sept 2024) and follow-ons**
- Sept 25, 2024: the FTC announced "Operation AI Comply" with five actions: DoNotPay ("world's first robot lawyer", settled for $193,000), Ascend Ecom, Ecommerce Empire Builders, FBA Machine/Passive Scaling, and Rytr (AI review-generator) — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/09/ftc-announces-crackdown-deceptive-ai-claims-schemes) **[prior knowledge, not re-verified this session]**; overview also in [BABL AI](https://babl.ai/ftc-cracks-down-on-deceptive-ai-claims-and-schemes-with-operation-ai-comply/)
- FBA Machine/Passive Scaling: the FTC alleges it defrauded consumers of more than $15 million. One tracker lists it as settled with a permanent business-opportunity ban — [Wiley Consumer Protection Download, Apr 7 2026](https://www.wiley.law/newsletter-Wiley-Consumer-Protection-Download-April-7-2026); [Vorp Labs federal AI enforcement tracker](https://vorplabs.com/ai-regulatory-updates/federal-enforcement)
- Ascend Ecom (June 2025): operators permanently banned from selling business opportunities. They must turn over assets toward a **$25 million** monetary judgment. The FTC alleged false claims that "cutting edge" AI-powered tools would let consumers quickly earn thousands of dollars a month in passive income — [FTC press release, June 2025](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/06/ftc-case-leads-order-banning-ascend-ecom-its-owners-business-opportunity-marketing); [MyChesCo](https://www.mychesco.com/?p=625971)
- Click Profit (Aug 2025): defendants permanently banned from the ecommerce business-opportunity industry. Judgments were **$13.6M** against Emslie, McGeoghean, Holton and affiliates and **$7.3M** against Masri and affiliates. The FTC said Click Profit falsely promised "guaranteed passive income using cutting-edge AI technology." Docket 1:25-cv-20973 (S.D. Fla.). Sources differ on whether the orders were final or proposed at announcement — [ABA Antitrust newsletter](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/antitrust_law/resources/newsletters/ftc-continues-crackdown-ai-deceit-e-commerce/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ftc-settles-with-e-commerce-scheme-operators-in-massive-fraud-case/)
- Ecommerce Empire Builders: status conflicts. One source says the FTC's tag page lists it as ongoing (TRO, receiver appointed). Another says it was permanently banned in mid-2025 — [FTC AI tag page via search](https://www.ftc.gov/industry/technology/artificial-intelligence); contradicted by [ABA newsletter](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/antitrust_law/resources/newsletters/ftc-continues-crackdown-ai-deceit-e-commerce/)
- Air AI: the FTC sued in Aug 2025 over false earnings claims and misrepresented performance of its "conversational AI" agents sold as business opportunities. In March 2026 a stipulated order (D. Ariz.) banned the company and owners from marketing business opportunities. One tracker reports an **$18M judgment, largely suspended, plus a $50,000 payment** — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/company-banned-marketing-business-opportunities-settle-ftc-charges-it-misled); [The Cannata Report](https://www.thecannatareport.com/ftc-to-ban-air-ai-from-marketing-biz-opps/); [Vorp Labs tracker](https://vorplabs.com/ai-regulatory-updates/federal-enforcement); [ai-first.company case summary](https://www.ai-first.company/evidence/cases/air-ai-ftc-ai-business-opportunity/)
- The common legal theory in these cases is the FTC Act Section 5 (deception), the Business Opportunity Rule (earnings claims need written substantiation and disclosure documents) and the Consumer Review Fairness Act (gag clauses on negative reviews). This is consistent across the press releases above, but the rule text was not re-fetched this session — [FTC Going into Business](https://www.ftc.gov/going-business)

**Investment / trading-bot side (CFTC, SEC)**
- CFTC customer advisory "AI Won't Turn Trading Bots into Money Machines" (Jan 26, 2024) says to ignore influencers or platforms claiming AI algorithms generate large, guaranteed returns, and that "AI technology can't predict the future." It cites Mirror Trading International, which falsely claimed a "bot" guaranteed at least 10% monthly returns — [CFTC learning resources](https://www.cftc.gov/learnandprotect/learningresources?combine=&field_article_or_advisory_topic_target_id=All&field_article_or_advisory_type_target_id=2486&items_per_page=25&page=0); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/cftc-taking-ai)
- CFTC advisory on generative-AI fraud (Mar 19, 2025) warns that GenAI makes fake trading platforms, profiles, voices and videos easier to produce — [The Financial Wire](https://thefinancialwire.com/an-ai-trading-bot-that-promises-steady-hands-off-profits-is-a-fast-growing-investment-scam/)
- SEC, NASAA and FINRA issued a joint investor alert on unregistered platforms claiming AI (e.g., "proprietary AI trading system that cannot lose"). A secondary source reports SEC charges in Dec 2025 against purported crypto trading platforms and "AI investment clubs" that recruited via social media and WhatsApp — [StockEducation](https://www.stockeducation.com/beginner-investing/ai-trading-bot-and-signal-scams/); [The Financial Wire](https://thefinancialwire.com/an-ai-trading-bot-that-promises-steady-hands-off-profits-is-a-fast-growing-investment-scam/)
- The SEC's first "AI-washing" actions were settled charges against advisers Delphia ($225,000) and Global Predictions ($175,000) in March 2024 for false AI claims — [SEC press release 2024-36](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2024-36) **[prior knowledge, not re-verified this session]**

**Course sellers vs. students**
- A search for independent investigations of Whop/Skool "make money with AI" sellers found none. Results were mostly seller marketing, e.g., a Skool seller claiming "$100K/month" at a "99% profit margin" and Whop listings with unverifiable testimonials ("$1,700 in the last 18 hours") — [Julian Goldie page](https://juliangoldie.com/make-money-with-skool-and-ai/); [Whop listing](https://whop.com/polished-intelligence/learn-to-make-money-with-ai-24/)
- A BlackHatWorld thread shows the economics from the seller's side. It plans a $99 Skool group teaching "make money with AI models" and projects $30k-$100k from 300-1,000 members before launch. The business is selling the method, not using it — [BlackHatWorld](https://www.blackhatworld.com/seo/journey-selling-a-course-skool-group-teaching-people-to-make-money-with-ai-models.1728169/)
- The FTC cases quantify consumer losses. Ascend Ecom was a $25M judgment, Click Profit about $20.9M combined, and FBA Machine more than $15M in alleged losses. Each was sold on "AI" passive-income promises. Students in these schemes typically paid tens of thousands for stores and coaching — sources as above ([FTC Ascend](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/06/ftc-case-leads-order-banning-ascend-ecom-its-owners-business-opportunity-marketing), [ABA](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/antitrust_law/resources/newsletters/ftc-continues-crackdown-ai-deceit-e-commerce/))

### Inferences
- The FTC theory is now well-worn: an "AI" label on a business opportunity plus earnings claims leads to a Section 5 / Business Opportunity Rule case, often a TRO, and then a lifetime industry ban. Enforcement continued under the 2025-2026 FTC leadership despite its stated lighter touch on AI regulation generally (Air AI order, March 2026). So "AI income" sellers who make specific earnings claims without substantiation are in a legal danger zone.
- The structural pattern in every FTC case is that defendants' revenue came from selling the opportunity (coaching, stores, software seats), not from the ecommerce or AI activity itself. That is the strongest available evidence that "the money is in the course."

### Gaps
- No platform-level data from Whop or Skool on what share of buyers of "AI income" products earn anything. No independent survey of student outcomes was found.
- 2026 FTC actions beyond Air AI and Ecommerce Empire Builders could not be enumerated because ftc.gov was unreachable. An unverified blog claim of Sept 2026 SEC charges against AI trading-bot schemes could not be confirmed.
- Final Ecommerce Empire Builders status conflicts between sources.

## 2. Cross-platform AI content rules (single view, with dates)

### Takeaway
No major platform bans AI content outright. All of them now (a) require or auto-apply labels for realistic synthetic media, often via C2PA metadata, and (b) demonetize or downrank mass-produced, repetitive, unoriginal output. That second rule is exactly what "one-prompt" pipelines produce. Monetization, not upload, is where the gate sits.

### Inferences
- The convergent rule across platforms is "AI is fine, mass production is not." Since "one-prompt, fully automated" is by definition mass production with minimal human input, it is the precise target of YouTube's inauthentic-content rule, Meta's unoriginal-content rule, TikTok's originality gate, Etsy's bot-listing ban, Deezer's takedowns and Google's scaled-content policy.
- Music is the clearest case of AI supply crushing returns: AI is more than half of new Deezer uploads but only 1-3% of streams, much of it fraudulent.

## 3. Legal landscape: copyright, training-data litigation, likeness/deepfake law, labeling mandates, advertising/reviews, tax

### Takeaway
In the US, purely AI-generated output is not copyrightable. The Supreme Court denied cert in Thaler on March 2, 2026, so "fully AI" assets give the seller no exclusive rights, and anyone can copy them. The big training-data cases (NYT v. OpenAI, Disney/Universal/WBD v. Midjourney, Getty v. Stability) remain unresolved on fair use as of Nov 2026, while music has moved to licensing settlements. New disclosure and likeness regimes are live: the EU AI Act Art. 50 since Aug 2, 2026, China's labeling rules since Sept 1, 2025, and the US TAKE IT DOWN Act with platform duties enforced from May 19, 2026. NO FAKES remains pending.

### Cited Findings
**Copyrightability of AI outputs**
- US Copyright Office, *Copyright and AI Part 2: Copyrightability* (Jan 29, 2025): prompts alone do not give sufficient human control. Purely AI-generated material is not protectable. Human selection/arrangement and modifications can be protected, and AI-assisted works remain registrable with AI portions disclaimed — [USCO Part 2 report](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf) **[prior knowledge, not re-verified this session]**
- Thaler v. Perlmutter: the D.C. Circuit (Mar 2025) held the Copyright Act requires a human author. **Supreme Court denied certiorari on March 2, 2026** (one source says Mar 3), without opinion. Thaler has exhausted appeals. How much human input is enough remains open — [Holland & Knight, Mar 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/the-final-word-supreme-court-refuses-to-hear-case-on-ai-authorship); [Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/ip-updates/supreme-court-declines-to-hear-thaler-v-perlmutter-leaving-human-authorship-requirement-intact.html); [Baker Donelson](https://www.bakerdonelson.com/supreme-court-denies-certiorari-in-thaler-v-perlmutter-ai-cannot-be-an-author-under-the-copyright-act); [Sterne Kessler](https://www.sternekessler.com/news-insights/client-alerts/ip-hot-topic-cert-denied-but-one-thing-is-cert-ain-the-human-authorship-requirement-for-copyright/)

**Training-data litigation affecting commercial users**
- NYT v. OpenAI & Microsoft (S.D.N.Y., Judge Stein): cross-motions for summary judgment on fair use were filed Sept 4, 2026, oppositions on Oct 23, and replies are due Nov 6, 2026. **No fair-use ruling and no trial date** as of late Oct 2026 — [AI Vortex, Oct 29 2026](https://www.aivortex.io/legal/ai-case-law/nyt-v-openai/); [AI Lawsuit Tracker](https://ailawsuittracker.com/cases/new-york-times-v-openai/)
- Disney/Universal (filed June 2025; Warner Bros. Discovery joined with its own suit, Sept 2025) v. Midjourney (C.D. Cal., Judge Kronstadt): pretrial. Midjourney filed a July 22, 2026 motion for judgment on the pleadings citing the Supreme Court's *Cox* decision against contributory-infringement claims. Fact discovery closed Sept 21, 2026 and the motions cutoff is Nov 23, 2026 (single tracker; verify docket) — [AI Lawsuit Tracker, Disney v. Midjourney](https://ailawsuittracker.com/blog/disney-v-midjourney-explained/); [Crail](https://www.crail.ai/blog/midjourney-disney-copyright-battle-status/); [Norton Rose Fulbright 2026 update](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/ce8eaa5f/ai-in-litigation-series-an-update-on-ai-copyright-cases-in-2026)
- Getty v. Stability AI (UK High Court, Nov 2025): Getty largely lost. The secondary copyright infringement claim (model as "infringing copy") was rejected and only limited trademark infringement was found (watermarks). Getty is appealing, with a Court of Appeal hearing expected late 2026/2027 and no appellate decision found. The US case (D. Del., 11,000+ works) is still pending — [McKool Smith, Nov 24 2025](https://www.mckoolsmith.com/newsroom-ailitigation-46); [Crail](https://www.crail.ai/blog/midjourney-disney-copyright-battle-status/); [Rinzara tracker](https://rinzara.com/articles/ai-copyright-lawsuit-tracker-2026)
- Bartz v. Anthropic: $1.5B class settlement (Sept 2025) over pirated books. Judge Alsup had held training on lawfully acquired books fair use but piracy-sourced libraries not — [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/ce8eaa5f/ai-in-litigation-series-an-update-on-ai-copyright-cases-in-2026) **[details from prior knowledge; NRF page not fetched]**
- Music labels v. Suno/Udio: **UMG settled with Udio (Oct 29/30, 2025)** and is building a licensed opt-in platform, not yet launched as of mid-2026. **Warner settled with Udio and with Suno (Nov 2025)**; Suno moves to licensed models in 2026 with download caps, and Suno acquired Songkick from WMG. **UMG v. Suno is unsettled** (talks at an impasse, Apr 2026). **Sony has settled with neither** and filed a new suit against Udio in July 2026 (30,117 Sony tracks reportedly in training data). The American Federation of Musicians sued UMG and Warner on June 5, 2026 over licensing members' recordings for AI training — [Digital Music News, Apr 9 2026](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/04/09/suno-universal-music-lawsuit-settlement-impasse/); [Law Commentary](https://www.lawcommentary.com/articles/suno-ai-music-copyright-lawsuit-licensing-deals); [Chartlex tracker](https://www.chartlex.com/blog/business/music-industry-ai-lawsuits-tracker-2026); [AI Vortex Suno/Udio](https://www.aivortex.io/legal/ai-case-law/suno-udio-music-ai/). Conflict: [Undetectr](https://undetectr.com/blog/suno-udio-licensing-deals-explained) claims Sony has preliminary agreements with both, which contradicts the other sources

**Right of publicity / deepfakes**
- Tennessee ELVIS Act (effective July 1, 2024) adds voice to the right of publicity and creates liability for tools whose primary purpose is unauthorized voice/likeness replication — [Tennessee Governor's office](https://www.tn.gov/governor/news/2024/3/21/photos--gov--lee-signs-elvis-act-into-law.html) **[prior knowledge, not re-verified]**
- NO FAKES Act (2026 version S. 4591 / H.R. 8915): reintroduced May 20, 2026 and passed the Senate Judiciary Committee unanimously on June 18, 2026. On the Senate calendar since June 24. A unanimous-consent request was reportedly blocked by Sen. Cruz on Sept 30, 2026 (free-speech objection). The House version is in committee. The 2026 text adds counter-notification, and most provisions would take effect 180 days after enactment. **Not law as of Nov 2026** — [GovTrack S. 4591](https://www.govtrack.us/congress/bills/119/s4591); [Holland & Knight, June 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/senate-judiciary-committee-advances-legislation-to-protect-name); [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/congress-reintroduces-the-no-fakes-act-what-s-new-in-the-2026-bill); [ByteBack Law, Aug 2026](https://www.bytebacklaw.com/2026/08/a-federal-shift-in-ai-and-the-right-of-publicity-no-fakes-act-advances-in-congress/); [Talkmie](https://talkmie.com/news/no-fakes-act-voice-likeness-us-status) (Cruz objection reported by secondary sources)
- TAKE IT DOWN Act (signed May 19, 2025) criminalizes publishing nonconsensual intimate imagery, including AI "digital forgeries." Platforms had to stand up notice-and-removal (48-hour takedown) by **May 19, 2026**, and the FTC is now enforcing. The FTC launched TakeItDown.ftc.gov for complaints. Civil penalties are reported at $53,088 per violation — [FTC press release, May 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-begins-enforcing-take-it-down-act); [WilmerHale, June 15 2026](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260615-the-take-it-down-act-goes-live); [CRS LSB11314](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11314); [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-May-19-Deadline-for-TAKE-IT-DOWN-Act-Compliance-Is-Your-Company-Prepared)

**Labeling mandates**
- EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations **applied from Aug 2, 2026** and were not delayed by the Digital Omnibus. They cover chatbot disclosure, machine-readable marking of synthetic output by providers, and deployer labeling of deepfakes and of AI text published to inform the public on matters of public interest. Exception: generative systems already on the market before Aug 2, 2026 have until **Dec 2, 2026** for marking/detection, with a reported further deferral to Feb 2, 2027 for watermark-detection interoperability. No retroactive labeling of pre-Aug 2026 content is required — [European Commission Art. 50 FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act); [Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content); [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-high-risk-deadline-omnibus-20260/); [Legalithm](https://www.legalithm.com/en/blog/eu-ai-act-what-applies-2-august-2026)
- Code of Practice on marking/labeling AI-generated content was published June 10, 2026, and Art. 50 guidelines were adopted July 20, 2026. The Commission/AI Board assessed the Code as adequate for showing compliance with Art. 50(2), (4), (5) — [Cobalt](https://www.cobalt.legal/news-cases/digital-omnibus-on-ai/); [European Commission FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act)
- Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force July 27, 2026) delayed **high-risk** obligations: Annex III to Dec 2, 2027 and Annex I to Aug 2, 2028. Art. 50 was not delayed — [Technology.org, Oct 13 2026](https://www.technology.org/2026/10/13/eu-ai-act-deadlines-after-the-digital-omnibus-what-applies-from-december-2026-to-august-2028/); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/)
- China: CAC "Measures for Labeling AI-Generated Synthetic Content" plus a mandatory national standard took effect **Sept 1, 2025**. They require explicit (visible) and implicit (metadata) labels, and platforms must detect and label — [CAC notice](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-03/14/c_1743654684782215.htm) **[prior knowledge, not re-verified this session]**

**Advertising, endorsements, fake reviews**
- FTC Trade Regulation Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (finalized Aug 14, 2024; effective Oct 21, 2024) bans fake reviews and testimonials, **explicitly including AI-generated ones** and reviews from people who don't exist or have no real experience. It also bans buying or selling them and undisclosed insider reviews, with civil penalties available — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials) **[prior knowledge, not re-verified]**
- Rytr (Operation AI Comply) was charged for selling an AI tool that generated unlimited fake reviews. This matters for any "AI review/testimonial generator" side hustle — [BABL AI](https://babl.ai/ftc-cracks-down-on-deceptive-ai-claims-and-schemes-with-operation-ai-comply/)
- The FTC Endorsement Guides (2023 revision) require clear disclosure of material connections, which applies to affiliate-heavy AI content and to virtual influencers — [FTC Endorsement Guides](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/endorsements-influencers-reviews) **[prior knowledge, not re-verified]**

**Tax**
- In the US, creator, affiliate, ad-share and digital-product income is self-employment income reportable on Schedule C. SE tax applies at net earnings of $400 or more. Platforms issue 1099-K/1099-NEC/1099-MISC, but income is taxable whether or not a form is issued — [IRS: self-employment tax](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employment-tax-social-security-and-medicare-taxes) **[prior knowledge, not re-verified]**
- 1099-K threshold: the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 2025) restored the $20,000 / 200-transaction threshold for third-party settlement organizations — [IRS 1099-K page](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/understanding-your-form-1099-k) **[prior knowledge, not re-verified; confirm current threshold]**
- EU: DAC7 (from 2023) requires digital platforms to report seller income to tax authorities **[prior knowledge; no source fetched]**

### Inferences
- The commercial implication of no copyright in purely AI output is that "fully AI" products (stock images, songs, books, faceless video) are legally copyable by competitors. The seller has no exclusive asset, only a platform account. That undercuts the "build a passive catalog" pitch.
- Training-data risk mostly sits with model vendors. But the Disney/Universal/WBD v. Midjourney complaint targets outputs depicting their characters. A user selling AI images, video or merch with recognizable IP faces direct infringement and trademark exposure regardless of how the fair-use training questions resolve.
- EU Art. 50 places marking duties on providers (the generators) and labeling duties on deployers who publish deepfakes or public-interest AI text. EU-facing AI content businesses publishing realistic synthetic video or news-like AI text now have a statutory labeling duty.

### Gaps
- No Court of Appeal date or ruling found for Getty v. Stability (UK).
- No outcome yet for any US fair-use summary judgment in a generative-output case (NYT, Midjourney).
- US state deepfake and political-ad AI laws (e.g., California AB 2655/2839, partly enjoined) were not researched this session.
- The UK's position (Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, copyright-AI consultation) was not researched.

## 4. Commercial-use terms of major generators

### Takeaway
Roughly, commercial rights come with paid plans. Free tiers of Midjourney, Suno, ElevenLabs, Kling and (per most sources) Runway are non-commercial and/or watermarked. Sora is gone: the consumer app closed Apr 26, 2026 and the API on Sept 24, 2026. Because US law gives no copyright in purely AI output, a "commercial license" from a vendor is permission and indemnity terms, not ownership protected against third parties.

### Cited Findings
- **Midjourney:** paid tiers (Basic, Standard, Pro, Mega) allow commercial use. Free/trial output falls under a non-commercial CC license. Companies with more than $1M revenue must use Pro/Mega (per Midjourney ToS) **[the $1M detail is prior knowledge]** — [terms.law Midjourney 2026](https://terms.law/2026/01/15/midjourney-commercial-use-rights-complete-2026-guide/); [terms.law FAQ](https://terms.law/FAQ/ai-tools/ai-commercial-use-faq.html)
- **Suno:** free tier has no commercial rights and Suno retains ownership. Paid tiers grant commercial use. The Warner settlement brings licensed models with download caps in 2026 — [licenseorg](https://www.licenseorg.com/blog/ai-commercial-use-guide-2026); [Dubspot](https://blog.dubspot.com/ai-music-licensing-explained-2026)
- **Udio:** after the UMG settlement Udio moved toward a walled-garden licensed platform. Download/export of tracks was restricted after the Oct 2025 deal **[prior knowledge; widely reported Oct-Nov 2025]** — [Dubspot](https://blog.dubspot.com/suno-udio-ai-music-lawsuits-2026); [Replayed Studio](https://replayedstudio.com/blog/suno-warner-udio-universal-deals/)
- **ElevenLabs:** free tier is non-commercial with attribution. One source says the Creator tier allows organic social and editorial use but not advertising, podcast or corporate use — [licenseorg](https://www.licenseorg.com/blog/ai-commercial-use-guide-2026) (single secondary source; verify against ElevenLabs terms)
- **Kling:** free tier is watermarked and personal-use only. Paid plans unlock commercial use but grant a usage right rather than clear ownership — [clipnova](https://www.clipnova.io/en/blog/best-ai-video-generators-with-commercial-rights); [licenseorg](https://www.licenseorg.com/blog/ai-commercial-use-guide-2026)
- **Runway:** sources conflict. One says the terms disclaim ownership of inputs/outputs with no free-plan carve-out. Others say the free plan is non-commercial, 480p and watermarked — [terms.law FAQ](https://terms.law/FAQ/ai-tools/ai-commercial-use-faq.html); contradicted by [layer3labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/best-ai-video-generators)
- **Google Veo:** commercial use allowed on paid plans and APIs. No clear statement was found on free AI Studio output. Output carries SynthID watermarks — [guptadeepak](https://guptadeepak.com/tools/top-5-ai-video-generation-tools-2026/); [rescroll](https://www.rescroll.ai/blog/best-free-ai-video-generators-2026)
- **Sora:** OpenAI announced discontinuation on March 24, 2026. The app/web closed April 26, 2026 and the API Sept 24, 2026. This also ended Disney's Dec 2025 deal ($1B investment and 200 characters licensed for Sora). Some aggregators claim Sora 2 remains accessible inside ChatGPT, which is unverified — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-sets-two-stage-sora-shutdown-with-app-closing-april-2026-and-api-following-in-september/); [Engadget](https://engadget.com/ai/openai-is-shutting-down-its-sora-video-generation-app-211023358.html); [Newsweek](https://newsweek.com/openai-scraps-sora-video-app-controversies-11731724)

### Inferences
- Many "one-prompt" tutorials still feature Sora or free tiers. A guide recommending free-tier Suno, ElevenLabs or Kling output for monetized channels is steering buyers into ToS violations. Any guide built on Sora is obsolete.

### Gaps
- Vendors' own current ToS pages were not fetched. All plan details are from secondary 2026 guides, some with commercial interests. Verify Runway, ElevenLabs and Veo in particular.

## 5. Creator-economy income distribution and AI slop prevalence

### Takeaway
Most creators earn little. About 4% earn more than $100k/yr per a widely cited Goldman Sachs estimate, and Linktree's survey found 46% of full-time creators earn under $1k. Meanwhile AI slop has flooded the supply side: about 21% of Shorts shown to a fresh YouTube account were AI slop, more than half of new Deezer uploads are fully AI, and roughly half of new English web articles were AI-generated by late 2024 (Graphite). More supply into fixed attention lowers the expected return per AI asset.

### Cited Findings
- Goldman Sachs estimate of about 50M creators globally, of whom about 4% earn more than $100k/yr. Brand deals are about 70% of creator revenue. Cited second-hand; no primary Goldman doc found — [Foundry](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/why-96-percent-of-creators-dont-make-a-living); [influencers.club](https://influencers.club/blog/creator-economy-statistics/)
- Linktree Creator Report (2022, n≈9,500): 46% of full-time creators earn under $1,000/yr and 12% earn $50,000 or more. Other compilations cite conflicting brackets — [MarTech Series](https://martechseries.com/technology/linktree-creator-report-reveals-key-insights-on-global-creator-economy/); [SmartCompany](https://smartcompany.com.au/finance/stream-of-revenue-online-creators-linktree-report). **Dated (2022).**
- Kapwing "AI Slop Report": a fresh account's first 500 YouTube Shorts were about **21% AI slop (104 videos)** and about **33% "brainrot" (165)**. Kapwing also identified 278 channels posting only AI slop among trending channels studied. Dates conflict: data collection was Oct 2025, published Nov 2025 per most coverage, while one source says Nov 28, 2024 — [Kapwing report](https://www.kapwing.com/blog/ai-slop-report-the-global-rise-of-low-quality-ai-videos/); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/one-in-five-youtube-shorts-shown-to-new-users-is-ai-generated-slop-study-finds/); [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/amount-ai-slop-brainrot-videos-213315059.html)
- Deezer: fully-AI tracks are more than 50% of daily uploads (June 2026 peak, ~90k/day) but 1-3% of streams. Up to 85% of streams on them were fraudulent — [Deezer newsroom](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/07/ai-music-exceeds-50-percent-daily-uploads-deezer/); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/deezer-half-uploads-ai-generated-streaming-fraud)
- Graphite (Oct 2025), analyzing about 65k English articles from Common Crawl: AI-generated articles surpassed human-written ones around Nov 2024 (about 50%) and then plateaued. It also found AI articles rarely appear in Google results or ChatGPT answers — [Graphite "More articles are now created by AI than humans"](https://graphite.io/five-percent/more-articles-are-now-created-by-ai-than-humans) **[prior knowledge, not re-verified this session; search did not surface it]**

### Inferences
- The combination of skewed creator earnings, AI content surging past 50% of supply in some channels, and platforms demonetizing mass production implies that the median return on "fully AI" content is near zero. Successful outliers typically involve real human curation, an audience or IP.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 update of the Linktree or Goldman figures found. No study measured earnings specifically of AI-content creators.
- Graphite numbers not re-verified this session.

## 6. Frequently marketed "fully AI" methods that are mostly scams or near-zero-EV

### Takeaway
The most heavily marketed "AI passive income" pitches are:
- AI trading bots with guaranteed returns
- "AI-powered" done-for-you Amazon/Walmart/Shopify stores
- "AI arbitrage" / AI agency-in-a-box resale (Air AI-style)
- "$10k/month with ChatGPT" courses
- AI fake-review/testimonial generation
- Mass-upload AI music, books or Shorts farms

Each maps to an FTC/CFTC/SEC enforcement theory, a platform anti-spam rule, or both.

### Cited Findings
- **AI trading bots / "AI investment clubs":** CFTC says AI can't predict markets and that guaranteed-return bot claims are red flags (e.g., Mirror Trading International's "10% monthly"). The SEC brought Dec 2025 charges against "AI investment clubs" recruited via social media/WhatsApp (secondary) — [CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/learnandprotect/learningresources?combine=&field_article_or_advisory_topic_target_id=All&field_article_or_advisory_type_target_id=2486&items_per_page=25&page=0); [The Financial Wire](https://thefinancialwire.com/an-ai-trading-bot-that-promises-steady-hands-off-profits-is-a-fast-growing-investment-scam/)
- **"AI-powered" done-for-you ecommerce stores:** Ascend Ecom ($25M), Click Profit (~$20.9M), FBA Machine (more than $15M) and Ecommerce Empire Builders all pitched AI-driven passive store income — [FTC Ascend](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/06/ftc-case-leads-order-banning-ascend-ecom-its-owners-business-opportunity-marketing); [ABA](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/antitrust_law/resources/newsletters/ftc-continues-crackdown-ai-deceit-e-commerce/)
- **"AI agency / resell AI agents" (AI arbitrage):** Air AI sold its conversational AI as a business opportunity with false earnings claims. It was banned from business-opportunity marketing (Mar 2026) — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/company-banned-marketing-business-opportunities-settle-ftc-charges-it-misled)
- **AI review generators:** Rytr was charged. The FTC fake-review rule bans AI-generated fake reviews — [BABL AI](https://babl.ai/ftc-cracks-down-on-deceptive-ai-claims-and-schemes-with-operation-ai-comply/)
- **AI music upload / stream farming:** Deezer reports up to 85% fraud on fully-AI streams and now removes such tracks. Spotify removed more than 75M spam tracks. A US criminal case (Michael Smith, N.D.N.Y. / S.D.N.Y., Sept 2024) charged a scheme using AI songs plus bots to collect about $10M in royalties — [Deezer](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/07/ai-music-exceeds-50-percent-daily-uploads-deezer/); Smith case: [DOJ SDNY press release](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/north-carolina-musician-charged-music-streaming-fraud-aided-artificial-intelligence) **[prior knowledge, not re-verified]**
- **Faceless AI Shorts/YouTube "cash cow" channels:** directly targeted by YouTube's July 2025 / July 2026 inauthentic-content rules — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)
- **"$10k/month with ChatGPT" courses on Whop/Skool/Gumroad:** revenue claims are unverified seller marketing. No independent outcome data exists, and the seller economics depend on member sales — [Whop listing](https://whop.com/polished-intelligence/learn-to-make-money-with-ai-24/); [BlackHatWorld](https://www.blackhatworld.com/seo/journey-selling-a-course-skool-group-teaching-people-to-make-money-with-ai-models.1728169/)

### Inferences
- Red-flag checklist the writer can use: guaranteed or specific income figures; "passive"/"hands-off" plus "AI"; upfront fees for stores or "AI agents"; refund-blocking or no-negative-review clauses (a CRFA violation); income proof only as screenshots; reliance on free-tier generator output for monetized use; and IP-infringing outputs (characters, celebrity voices).
- Comparatively viable AI-assisted (not "fully AI") methods are those with human creative contribution, disclosure, paid-tier licenses and real customers, such as client services that use AI tools. These sit outside both the copyright gap and the platform mass-production bans. This conclusion is drawn from the rules above, not from earnings data.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data found on loss rates or refund rates for AI side-hustle course buyers.
- No 2026-specific CFTC/SEC AI-bot enforcement verified from primary sources.
